# ASO (App Store Optimization) Keyword Strategy

Real discoverability work, not growth-hack tricks — this is what
actually drives organic downloads for both stores' search algorithms.

## Keyword tiers to target

**Tier 1 — high intent, exam-specific (your real differentiation, less
competitive than generic "learn Spanish"):**
"IELTS prep app", "DELF B2 practice", "HSK vocabulary app", "JLPT N4
study", "TOPIK practice test", "German exam Goethe prep" — these
searches come from people with a concrete goal and higher willingness
to pay, and Duolingo doesn't rank strongly here because it isn't
built for it.

**Tier 2 — language + skill combos:**
"[language] speaking practice", "[language] listening test", "learn
[language] flashcards" — moderate competition, but your AI-scored
speaking practice is a genuine feature hook worth owning in these
results.

**Tier 3 — underserved language head terms:**
"learn Thai app", "learn Tagalog", "Georgian language app" — lower
search volume individually, but far less competition, and this is
where you can realistically rank #1-3 rather than fighting Duolingo/
Babbel/Busuu for page one.

## Where keywords actually matter
- **Apple**: title, subtitle, and the dedicated 100-character keyword
  field (not visible to users, but indexed) — front-load the highest-
  intent Tier 1 terms here since space is limited.
- **Google Play**: title, short description, and full description are
  all indexed — Play's algorithm weighs description content more than
  Apple's, so natural keyword density in the full description matters
  more here than on iOS.

## Localized ASO
Don't just translate your English listing — research native-language
search terms directly (e.g. what a Japanese speaker searching for
English exam prep actually types isn't a literal translation of "IELTS
prep app"). Do this per-market for at least your top 4-5 launch
languages before assuming a translated listing performs the same as a
locally-researched one.

## Review/rating strategy (legitimate version)
- In-app prompt to rate, triggered after a genuine positive moment
  (completing a mock exam, hitting a streak milestone) — never
  immediately on open, and never gated so only happy users see the
  prompt while unhappy users are redirected to a feedback form instead
  (this pattern is against both stores' guidelines and is worth
  avoiding on principle, not just compliance).
- Respond to reviews, especially critical ones about specific
  languages — this signals active development to both the algorithm
  and prospective users browsing reviews.
