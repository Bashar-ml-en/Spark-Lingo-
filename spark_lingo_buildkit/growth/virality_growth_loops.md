# Virality & Growth Loops — Grounded Version

"Going viral" isn't a switch to flip; it's what happens when sharing is
a natural byproduct of using the product. Here's what to actually build,
roughly in order of effort-to-impact.

## 1. Shareable milestones (highest effort-to-impact ratio)
- Streak milestones, mock exam score improvements, and
  placement-test → current-level progress are all genuinely
  screenshot-worthy moments if designed well (the `score_band_gauge`
  widget from Prompt 07 should be built with this in mind — a clean,
  attractive visual, not just a progress bar).
- A one-tap "share my IELTS practice band score" card generator
  (branded, but not obnoxiously so) turns a personal achievement into
  organic exposure. This works because the sharer has a genuine reason
  to share (pride in progress), not because the app is nagging them to.

## 2. Referral loop tied to a real incentive
- Give both sides something (e.g. extra mock exam credits for
  referrer + referee) rather than only rewarding the referrer — two-
  sided incentives convert better and feel less exploitative.
- Keep this optional and low-pressure — a referral prompt after a
  positive moment (see ASO doc's review-prompt timing logic; same
  principle applies here).

## 3. Study-buddy / accountability mechanics
- Optional: pairing or small-group streak tracking for people preparing
  for the same exam sitting (e.g. "3 people you know are also prepping
  for the June DELF B2 session"). This taps a real behavior — people
  studying for the same exam already form study groups organically;
  giving them a lightweight in-app version increases retention and
  organic word-of-mouth within existing social circles rather than
  manufacturing virality from nothing.

## 4. Content marketing (compounds slowly, real ROI)
- Short "IELTS Band 7 writing example, annotated" or "HSK 4 vocabulary
  you'll actually be tested on" content pieces, published where your
  target users already search (YouTube Shorts, TikTok, blog/SEO) —
  this feeds directly back into the ASO Tier 1 keywords, since people
  who watch a DELF prep video are exactly who searches "DELF prep app"
  next.

## What NOT to build
- Fake urgency ("only 3 spots left in this cohort") when there's no
  real scarcity — both stores flag this, and it damages trust with
  exactly the outcome-focused users you're targeting.
- Aggressive push notification cadences designed to manufacture
  re-opens rather than serve a real learning need (e.g. spaced-
  repetition due-card reminders are legitimate; generic "come back!"
  pings with no content reason are not, and drive uninstalls).
- Paywalling the referral/sharing mechanic itself — sharing should be
  frictionless for every user, not a premium feature.
